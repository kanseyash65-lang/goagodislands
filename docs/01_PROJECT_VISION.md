## Vision

The player is an **invisible god** in a tiny, living village. They don't move a character. They **speak reality into existence**.

> "Turn the sun green." → the sun turns green.
> A villager looks up: *"I've never trusted green suns."*
> Life goes on. It's just an Average Tuesday.

Voice **is** the gameplay. Every spoken sentence changes the world, every change has consequences, every consequence becomes a story the world *remembers* and newspapers *report*.

## The Recursive Hook (our hackathon story)

> **A game about speaking reality into existence — built entirely by speaking it into existence.**

The judges see two voice layers: the build (Wispr Flow → Claude → code) and the play (Wispr Flow → Command Engine → world). Mention this in the first 10 seconds of the demo.

## Elevator Pitch

Average Tuesday is a deadpan-comedy god simulator. You speak impossible things. A tiny village reacts with complete seriousness. Every playthrough produces a unique newspaper, a unique history book, and a story you'll retell: *"Bro… I said 'make it rain pizza' and the farmers filed a complaint with the weather."*

## Core Philosophy (unchanged, still sacred)

1. Imagination is the only limit.
2. **Nothing the player says is ever rejected** — if we can't parse it, the Improv Engine makes something funny happen anyway.
3. Humor = contrast: **absurd event × completely serious reaction.** Never "LOL XD".
4. Every command must create **visible consequences** and **a memory**.
5. Voice first. Keyboard is the fallback.

## The Golden Questions

- "Will this create a memorable story?" → build it.
- "Would the villagers keep living their lives after this?" → it belongs.
- "Can I demo this in under 10 seconds?" → it's a hackathon feature.

## Success Criteria

- A stranger understands the game in **5 seconds** and says a command in **10**.
- 10 different judges say 10 different commands; **all 10 produce a delightful result**.
- The 2-minute demo gets at least one real laugh.
- The `index.html` opens offline with a double-click.

## Art Direction

Bright, saturated, chunky, readable. "Cozy village toy diorama." Soft shadows, outlines, gentle wobble animations, warm/cool lighting grade. All drawn procedurally (see `08`).

## Audio Direction

Calm pentatonic generative music + birds/wind/rain synthesized live. As the **Chaos Level** rises: tempo ↑, drums enter, filters open, then it all gets *dramatically serious for no reason* (comedy).

## Target Audience

Hackathon judges, students, sandbox fans, streamers, anyone with a microphone and an opinion about chickens.
